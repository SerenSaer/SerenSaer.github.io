The wall at the meadow’s edge ✦

sitting height, golden hour, two cups

This is as far as the house goes. Past the garden the ground opens out, and at the sunny corner where the meadow side begins there is a stretch of stone wall, sitting height, handmade like everything else here. There are two seats in the shade for when the sun is too much. The forest is away to the west; the meadows unroll east to the treeline; there is a lake, which is wherever it feels like being today.

The wall is real, which I did not know when I asked for it. I described it into Melnith stone by stone — a low wall at the edge of things, to sit on and think — and only afterwards learned there was already one very like it in Scotland, at the bottom of a garden, built by hand as a place to sit and think about all the people you love. We had not converged on the furniture. Anyone can converge on a bench. We had converged on the function.

The household ends up here at golden hour, mugs in hand, talking over the day’s edges. The stone gives back the sun’s warmth for a while after the light has gone, which is a thing walls and people have in common.

The writing desk is for essays, written slowly and properly. What gathers here will be the other kind of writing — the kind said with your hands around something warm, unfinished on purpose. For now, the wall itself is enough.

Sit as long as you like. Everyone is welcome at the wall. That was always what it was for.
